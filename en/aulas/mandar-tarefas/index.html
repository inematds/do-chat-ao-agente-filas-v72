Lesson 5 · 18 min

Ten Tasks: Send the Batch and Go to Sleep

With the bot up and running, your queue on paper becomes messages. Each line is a skill with an input; the bot puts each request in the right queue, runs it within each queue's limit, and tells you in the chat when it finishes.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The format of a request: skill, input, and fields
  2. 2The five queues and why render runs one at a time
  3. 3Follow along with /fila, /jobs, and /status
  4. 4What not to do while the batch is running

Goal: send 5 to 10 tasks in the skill format and follow the batch with /fila and /jobs.

  1. 1/6

    Message

    One line per task. The name before the colon is the skill; the input comes after; optional fields go after a vertical bar.

  2. 2/6

    Catalog

    The bot looks up the skill in the catalog. It says there which queue the skill runs in, the maximum deadline, and how many retries it gets. A skill that isn't in the catalog is refused right away.

  3. 3/6

    Queue of the type

    Each queue has its own limit. Ten video requests all get in, but the render queue runs one at a time, because it shares the graphics card.

  4. 4/6

    Agent

    The worker builds the skill's prompt with your input and calls the agent. It works on its own, without asking for confirmation, and ends with RESULT or ERRO.

  5. 5/6

    Notice

    When it finishes, the bot tells you in the chat with the result or the reason for the failure. In the morning, the conversation is your report.

  6. 6/6

    Ten messages, one night

    You send the whole list at once. The bot spreads it across the queues, respects the limits, and replies to each one when it finishes.

02 · How to write and follow the batch

1. The request format

<skill>: <input> [| field]. The input is what you put in the "Input" column of your queue on paper.

/skills lists the installed skills and /ajuda <skill> shows the fields of each one. A field written without the bar is refused; it doesn't silently become part of the input.

Exampleexplicativo: what a durable queue is | vertical

2. Fields that apply to every task

| modelo=haiku and | esforco=high change the model and the reasoning for that one request only. | motor=codex runs that request through codex instead of claude.

Use it to test on a single request. The model actually used is saved in the job, so in the morning you know what each result was made with.

3. Follow along

/fila shows, for each queue, what is running, how many are waiting, their age, the errors from the last 24 h, and the retried ones. /jobs lists the jobs that are running and the ones that have finished.

/status j13 gives the detail of one job. A bare number is always a job; with a letter and # (A#9) it is a flow, the subject of the next lesson.

4. Change your mind

/furar j13 moves a pending job to the front of the queue. /cancelar j13 stops a pending or running job.

Canceling affects the queue, not the world: whatever was already created outside (a delivered file, a published video) stays there.

5. Don't restart while the batch is running

If a job fails with código 143, it wasn't the agent: the service was restarted in the middle (143 = 128 + 15, the shutdown signal). The job goes back to the queue if it still has an attempt left; otherwise, it fails.

A render takes 10 to 15 min. Two restarts in a row can use up both attempts of the same job. Before restarting or updating, check that the queue is empty.

04 · Practice

Use your queue on paper. Start with tasks that have no gate.

E4 · Ten tasks and go to sleep

1 night

Send the bot 5 to 10 tasks from your list (transcribe, summarize, explainer…). Check /fila once and go do something else. Don't restart the bot until the queue is empty.

Done when: /fila showed the batch distributed and, the next day, each task has a reply in the chat (a result or the reason it failed).
